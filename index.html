Flagship · Catastrophe modelling

India Multi-Peril Catastrophe Model — Flood & Cyclone

I built a probabilistic CAT model for Indian flood and cyclone risk from scratch in R to quantify India's insurance protection gap.

  • Flood hazard as Poisson(λ=7) frequency with lognormal severity, calibrated to NDMA Annual Reports (2000–2023)
  • Cyclone hazard as Poisson(λ=1.67) landfalling storms, calibrated to the IMD cyclone e-atlas
  • Vulnerability curves for four Indian construction types — RCC, brick, semi-pucca, kutcha
  • 3-layer per-occurrence XL reinsurance structure (₹100 Cr retention, ₹1,000 Cr limit)

Case in point: my model rates Cyclone Fani (2019) as roughly a 1-in-2-year event — not a modelling error, but a sign of how far insured losses (~₹1,200 Cr) trail true economic damage (~₹30,000 Cr) in India.

Loss reserving

Loss Reserve Estimation — Indian MET Lines

I built Chain Ladder and Bornhuetter–Ferguson from first principles for Motor, Engineering and Treaty lines, with a method-selection framework and a companion Shiny dashboard.

  • Synthetic triangles calibrated to IRDAI and GIC Re annual reports (2022–23)
  • Chain Ladder (Mack 1993) + Bootstrap CL, and BF anchored to a 70% a priori ELR
  • Finding: Chain Ladder overstates Engineering reserves — Kerala floods (2018) and Cyclone Fani (2019) inflate the development ratios it extrapolates from
  • Finding: Indian Motor TP develops far slower than UK benchmarks (~45% emerged by Year 2 vs ~70% in the UK) due to MACT tribunal delays
